# Epilogue — "Hello, World 2.0"

**Purpose:** full circle, warm exit, and the handoff — from Evergreen to the
kids, and from the kids to the reader.

**Core jokes:** the Ch0 museum assistant, scandalized: "you mean you actually
SAY 'Hello World'?!" · Evergreen's "attention depleted" exit line, finally
meaning something warm · the high-score table's all-time champion.

---

## Story draft

The following semester, for the first time in forty years, Archaeology
Principles: Controlled Silicon of the Past required a lottery.

Three hundred names for thirty chairs. The Registrar's office called it a
statistical anomaly. Prof. Evergreen called it "an adequate turnout," and
bought a new tie, and told no one, and everyone knew.

Flamey and Starlax sat at the front now, facing the wrong way — teaching
assistants get the worst view of the board and the best view of the faces.
The faces were the good part. Thirty kids and one auditing museum assistant,
who had asked to enroll the day after the campus came back on and had
attended every session since with the expression of a man discovering that
his house had always had a basement.

In the hallway outside the classroom stood a machine in a wooden cabinet, on
permanent loan from the museum, polished to a shine: CATVENTURE, restored,
complete, playable. Its high-score table sorted itself proudly, merge-style.
The all-time champion, forty thousand points clear of second place, was
`CA7` — which everyone assumed was one of the kids being funny, except that
Flamey and Starlax had checked the input logs, twice, and had quietly agreed
never to check them again.

"Today," said Prof. Evergreen, to the fullest room of his life, "we begin
with a question. Who can tell me how you create a game from scratch?"

A new kid in the second row raised her hand. "You just say 'Hello World'
and—"

"Ah," said Prof. Evergreen, and looked at his teaching assistants, and
stepped aside from the board.

Flamey stood. He picked up the stylus. In the language that was never lost —
the language running quietly inside the walls and the doors and the cabinet
in the hall and his own warm chest, sixty times a second, forever — he wrote
the first line. The oldest line. The line every builder writes first, in
every century, to make sure someone is listening:

`print("Hello, World!")`

He pressed RUN.

`Hello, World!` said the screen.

"WAIT," said the museum assistant, half out of his chair. "Wait wait wait.
You mean you actually — you actually SAY 'Hello World'?! It's REAL? It's a
real INSTRUCTION?"

"It's a real instruction," said Starlax, grinning. "Everything is. That's the
whole secret. There's no magic down there. It's better than magic. It's just—"

"Numbers," said thirty kids and one museum assistant and one teenage robot,
all together, like a heartbeat.

Prof. Evergreen stood at the side of his full classroom and let the noise of
questions rise — real questions, why questions, the kind he had waited forty
years to run out of time for. He checked his watch. He gathered his notes.
At the door he paused, and the room quieted, because by now everyone knew
the words that were coming, and nobody knew he had never once meant them the
way they sounded.

"As science has shown," said the last keeper of the lost language of the
machines, looking back at all of it — the kids, the code, the cabinet
glowing in the hall, the cat asleep on the warm exact center of the master
console — "your attention must be depleted."

It wasn't. It never was again.

"Class," he said, "is yours."

THE END

## Mini-game spec: "New Game?"

- **Not a level — a door.** After the credits, the reader's finished game
  unlocks completely: free play, plus a simple editor exposing everything
  they built — the sprite grid (Ch4), palette (Ch3), sounds (Ch5), rules
  (Ch10), enemy brain (Ch12), even the loop's speed (Ch9).
- **The pitch:** the book ends and the reader keeps building. Change the
  hero. Change the rules. Break it. Debug it. There is no goal screen because
  the goal is now theirs.
- **Share:** export the cartridge as a small file (it fits in 32 KB — Ch6
  wasn't decoration) that friends with the book can load. Trading games in a
  32 KB file, 500 years in the future, exactly like the ancients did.
- **Final easter egg:** typing the collected hex food-words (`CA7F00D` etc.)
  as a cheat code unlocks Nova as a playable character. She is slower than
  the cat, refuses to jump, and wins anyway. Statistically, she was due.
